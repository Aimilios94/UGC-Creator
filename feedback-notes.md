## Round 2 — making the landing example assets (19 Nov 2026)
Made outside the app with Nano Banana Pro (image, edit) and Kling v3 Pro (image-to-video) on fal; lessons for the M2 video pipeline:
- Kling's multi-shot prompt (three shots in one call) made the subject jump between places. → one continuous take with a start frame and an end frame keeps the person and the setting consistent; prefer it for scene clips.
- Two 15-second takes both failed on ball physics: the ball vanished and no clear goal was shown. → storyboards should avoid beats that depend on object physics (a shot going in, a throw being caught); the M3 judge's drift check should sample the frames where they happen.
- Background boards and signs came out with made-up lettering in every stadium and street scene. → confirms the M1 finding: on-screen text must come from `compose.py`, never from the model.
- With the product photo as the reference, the edit model kept the product the same across scenes (basketball stripe, keyboard case, hoodie back print). → supports [D10](DECISIONS.md) (edit from the photo, not text-to-image).
- An edit model keeps the style of its input (a stylised image stays stylised), and asking it for empty space at the top added a pasted band. → restyle with a fresh generation; leave room for text by cropping in code.
- A highlight cut in ffmpeg (two segments with a 0.3 s dissolve) hid a failed moment at no cost. → `compose.py` can trim a weak span of a clip instead of paying for a re-render.

## Round 4 — the owner's real-AI storyboard test (26 Nov 2026)
Local stack with the real AI (owner's go, 0.10 EUR limit, storyboards only, no pictures or video).

**Ad 1: a detailed, timed sports story** (20 s, 4 parts written as `[0:00-0:05] …`, spoken lines in quotes, a logo on black at the end; a running-shoe photo; style left on "realistic"). Cost 0.0188 USD ≈ 0.017 EUR (photo read, slot filler, writer, continuity).
- **Worked:** 4 scenes of 5 + 6 + 5 + 4 s, as timed; no questions (right for a detailed story); the spoken lines went into the voiceover word for word; on-screen text stayed empty; suggestions were sensible, with no invented claims (the hooks use only the brief's grip, breathability and support); every clip 1,259–1,720 characters, within 2,310, with the user's scene text in full.
- **Square-bracket times leave debris:** each scene text starts with `0:00-0:05]` and ends with `[`, and the notes end with `[`. All of it reaches the clips.
- **"From your story" is too loose:** the runner's look ("short dark hair, stubble") and clothes ("dark athletic jacket, training tights") were marked "from your story", but the story only says "a runner". The camera style "smartphone UGC feel" was also marked "from your story", while the story asks for a cinematic, anamorphic look. The code check needs only one story word in the value.
- **The style choice contradicts the story:** the brief's default "realistic" adds "Realistic phone footage, natural light." to every clip; the story asks for cinematic, high-contrast pre-dawn light.
- **"Rapid cuts" inside one scene:** scene 2 is three different shots (chalk, rain on a court, a swimmer) in one clip, while the clip rules say "One continuous take, no cuts". Only "cut to" splits a scene today. The writer's timeline also says "fast cut to" and "jump cut".
- **A logo scene went to the video AI:** scene 4 ("hard cut to black as a white logo slams onto screen") is text and a logo, a Kling weak spot that code did not catch; the negative prompt says "no logo overlay" at the same time. The app's own end card is the right place for it. The spoken line keeps the placeholder `[Brand Name]`, which the voice would read aloud.
- **Sound and spoken words reach the video AI:** "audio: sharp snap of shoelaces…" and `voiceover says: "Nobody claps at 5 AM…"` are in the clip text; Kling makes no sound, and quoted words can invite moving lips or lettering.
- **Start and end lines cut mid-phrase:** "…glisten under.", "…cool side key and faint.", "…sweat and." (cut to the budget, then a full stop added).
- **Negatives worded as "no …":** the sheet's "never" ("no bright daytime, no comedic or staged smiles") goes into the negative prompt as "no bright daytime": a double negative in a field that already means "avoid".
- **One place for every scene:** "pre-dawn outdoor stadium, wet asphalt basketball court, gym and pool" goes into all four clips; no scene got its own place.
- **The story check asked once more for nothing:** it read "Dynamic" and "Hard" (sentence starts after `[0:11-0:16]`) and "Name" (from `[Brand Name]`) as names. About half the slot filler's cost (≈0.004 USD) went on the second call, and the result was the same.
