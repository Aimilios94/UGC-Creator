## Round 2 — making the landing example assets (19 Nov 2026)
Made outside the app with Nano Banana Pro (image, edit) and Kling v3 Pro (image-to-video) on fal; lessons for the M2 video pipeline:
- Kling's multi-shot prompt (three shots in one call) made the subject jump between places. → one continuous take with a start frame and an end frame keeps the person and the setting consistent; prefer it for scene clips.
- Two 15-second takes both failed on ball physics: the ball vanished and no clear goal was shown. → storyboards should avoid beats that depend on object physics (a shot going in, a throw being caught); the M3 judge's drift check should sample the frames where they happen.
- Background boards and signs came out with made-up lettering in every stadium and street scene. → confirms the M1 finding: on-screen text must come from `compose.py`, never from the model.
- With the product photo as the reference, the edit model kept the product the same across scenes (basketball stripe, keyboard case, hoodie back print). → supports [D10](DECISIONS.md) (edit from the photo, not text-to-image).
- An edit model keeps the style of its input (a stylised image stays stylised), and asking it for empty space at the top added a pasted band. → restyle with a fresh generation; leave room for text by cropping in code.
- A highlight cut in ffmpeg (two segments with a 0.3 s dissolve) hid a failed moment at no cost. → `compose.py` can trim a weak span of a clip instead of paying for a re-render.

## Round 3 — the owner's manual tests of M2 (20–21 Nov 2026)
On the local stack, first with mock providers, then with the real AI (owner's go, 5 EUR limit; about 2.16 EUR spent over two real videos, [D29](DECISIONS.md)).
- The owner wants to steer: use their own story, pick the opening line, use pictures they already have, choose clip lengths, go back a step. → [D27](DECISIONS.md).
- "What we see" looked like it drove the picture, but the image model reads the hidden image prompt; one- or two-line prompts are too thin for a good clip. → detailed image prompts and timed motion shot lists, shown as "Show what the AI gets" (D27, D28).
- The story must lead: scene count and lengths should follow the user's own structure (4 shots of 2 s), not a fixed 3. → 1–5 scenes (D28); timestamps read in code after the real writer merged two beats (D29).
- Jargon and unclear choices confused: "Approve and make the images", "What to make", prices without parts, "Up to 40 s", "Every scene starts from your own picture", hook formulas. → plain wording, prices by part with the model names, explained limits, "First line of the ad" (D28, D29).
- Without a length, the AI cannot know how many scenes to make. → "Video length" with about 2.5 s per scene, and a recommendation (Apply/Keep/Undo) that joins scenes without cutting the story (D29).
- **First real video:** the look was good, but every scene change was obvious: each clip restarted from its own picture. → one continuous shot (each scene starts from the previous cut), a 0.3 s blend, and a continuity rule in every clip instruction (D29); to be re-tested with the real AI.
- **Second real video** (the owner's extra test: 2 scenes, the product photo in both): the cut still jumped, because a scene with its own picture started from that picture; continuous chaining only applied to "Generate" scenes. → every scene flows into the next whatever pictures are given: the clip before an own picture ends on it (Kling's end frame), a continuity check writes how each scene starts and ends, and the cut type follows (clean, 0.1 s, or 0.3 s for separate shots) ([D30](DECISIONS.md)); to be re-tested with the real AI.
- **Pasting the full 7,279-character styled prompt** cut it at 4,000 characters without a word, so the timeline was lost; the video AI also reads only 2,500 characters per clip. → up to 12,000 characters with a counter, never a silent cut; each clip gets its scene word for word plus the notes in full or shortened for that scene; "Shorten it for me" for a scene too long on its own; the exact clip text in "Show what the AI gets" ([D30](DECISIONS.md)).
- Long prompts with style, setting and light before the timeline should stay together and apply to every scene. → optional "Style and world notes" (D29).
- **Third real video, the D30 test** (21 Nov 2026, 15:14 UTC: the 20 s styled prompt, 5 × 4 s, own picture first, then four chained scenes; 1.60 EUR): all 4 cuts are clean and match (mean frame difference 3.8–11.2 of 255, measured from the findings archive). Found: the first picture was not 9:16, so it was fitted with a blurred fill, and Kling animated the blurred bands into every chained scene. → fixed the same day: an own picture a crop would cut by more than a quarter is extended to the full frame by Nano Banana instead of a blurred fill ([D31](DECISIONS.md)). Checked with the real AI on 22 Nov 2026 (one picture, about 0.036 EUR): the square hoodie photo came back as one seamless 9:16 photo, no blur or bars; the model re-draws the scene a little (the hoodie slightly smaller, the embroidery slightly softer), worth watching on products with fine print. Also found in the review: with the real writer, Kling gets the writer's rewrite of each scene, not the user's words → the strategy plan's clip builder sends the user's words ([D31](DECISIONS.md)).
- Local test pitfalls: the mock clips showed placeholders (now your own pictures with a slow move), mock renders count toward the local daily limit, and Vite in Docker needs `docker compose restart frontend` after code changes (SETUP).

- **The real slot filler, checked live** (22 Nov 2026, golden 01 and 02, 0.0085 EUR): it marked honestly (only words found in the user's text were "yours"), read 3 paragraphs into 3 scenes in the user's words, and asked sensible questions (none about what the prompt already said). But it invented product facts the user never gave ("16 oz", "screw lid") as guesses, and wrote long camera guesses with their own times. → the slot filler may only guess visible details, never product facts; camera guesses are short; the writer writes the timeline ([D31](DECISIONS.md) phase 4).
- **Golden 03 live** (22 Nov 2026, 0.0201 EUR over two runs): with the weak-spot rule only in the instructions, the real writer kept "he throws the hoodie to his friend" and timed the hoodie flying; the photo reader's "laid flat on a wooden background" reached the clip of a worn hoodie. → weak spots found in code and a missed swap asked once more; the photo reader describes the product only. The second run swapped to a hand-over on the first call ([D31](DECISIONS.md)).
- **The owner's storyboard review** (22 Nov 2026, mock stack): "Cast and world" showed a bright room at home for a story set in Shibuya, and a voiceover and on-screen text the owner did not want reached the video when not deleted. → story first, checked in code ("from your story"), the mock reads the story; voiceover and on-screen text start empty with suggestions ([D31](DECISIONS.md)).
